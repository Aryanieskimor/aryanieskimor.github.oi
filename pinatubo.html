The 1991 Historic Eruption

On June 15, 1991, Mount Pinatubo produced the second-largest volcanic eruption of the 20th century. The eruption injected roughly 20 million tons of sulfur dioxide ($SO_2$) into the stratosphere.

This stratospheric aerosol layer reflected incoming solar radiation, causing average global temperatures to drop by approximately $0.5^\circ\text{C}$ ($0.9^\circ\text{F}$) from 1991 to 1993.

Modern Ecology & Eco-Tourism

The collapse of the volcano's summit created a $2.5\text{ km}$ wide crater filled with rainfall, forming Lake Pinatubo. Today, it is a protected eco-tourism trail managed in cooperation with local Indigenous Aeta communities.
