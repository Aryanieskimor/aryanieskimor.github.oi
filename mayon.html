Geological Overview

Mount Mayon is an active stratovolcano famed for its symmetric "perfect cone" shape. Located in the province of Albay in the Bicol Region, it forms the center of Mayon Volcano Natural Park.

Its steep slopes are formed by overlapping layers of basaltic-andesitic lava flows, volcanic ash, and pyroclastic debris from continuous eruptive cycles.

Historical Significance & Safety Awareness

Mayon's most catastrophic eruption occurred on February 1, 1814, destroying the nearby town of Cagsawa. Today, the surviving Cagsawa Church belfry serves as a historic reminder of volcanic hazards.

Continuous monitoring by PHIVOLCS ensures local communities receive real-time alerts regarding pyroclastic density currents (PDCs), lahars, and active lava effusion.
